# PCOptopus commercial demo VO — one line per frame (01..08).
# Plain language. Assume the viewer is not technical.

01|Meet PCOptopus — an app that watches over your Mac the way a checkup watches over your health. Its score is called Pulse. One hundred means your computer is in great shape.
02|Right now Pulse is eighty-four. That means something is holding your Mac back. Here, it's running low on free space — like a closet that's too full to close. One button starts the fix: Fight for a hundred.
03|PCOptopus looks for old leftover files you probably don't need anymore, and shows you a clear list with sizes. Important: it never throws anything away by itself. It only recommends. You stay in control.
04|When it does change a setting, it writes it down in a Journal — what it was before, what it is now. Changed your mind? Hit Undo, and it puts things back.
05|Before it can change anything, you have to give permission in the Contract. By default, that list is empty — so if you never say yes, it never touches your system. Safety first.
06|This screen is simply your Mac's ID card — the model and chip — so you always know what machine you're taking care of.
07|And if you ever want everything to stop, Settings has a kill switch. One click, and PCOptopus stands down.
08|So that's the idea: a simple health score for your Mac, gentle recommendations, and nothing happens without your say-so. Keep Pulse at a hundred — and keep your computer feeling fast and trustworthy.
